# Fleek

> London-based B2B wholesale marketplace for secondhand and vintage clothing. Suppliers (mostly sorting hubs in Pakistan, India and Dubai) sell graded bundles, bales and handpicks to resellers and shops (mostly in the US and Europe). Fleek is building its own AI grading model, Fleek Sort. Site: [joinfleek.com](https://www.joinfleek.com/) · Help centre: [support.joinfleek.com](https://support.joinfleek.com/hc/en-us) · Supplier app: [Fleek Vendor (iOS)](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/fleek-vendor/id1621179362) · LinkedIn: [company/joinfleek](https://www.linkedin.com/company/joinfleek)

Research date: 26 Sep 2026. Fleek is also the venue (Fleek HQ, 20–22 Commercial St, London E1).

Not to be confused with **Fleek (fleek.co)**, a web hosting/web3 company with a GraphQL API, or **getfleek.app**, a subscriptions/rewards app. Both are unrelated companies that share the name.

## What it is

Fleek connects wholesale suppliers of used clothing with people who resell it. The founding story (Fortune): a Brick Lane vintage shop closed after being scammed out of more than $15,000 by an online broker. Sourcing trips had stopped during COVID, and brokers charged high commissions. Fleek's pitch is trusted, direct access to verified suppliers, with Fleek standing behind each transaction.

- **Suppliers:** rag houses, graders, wholesalers, donation centres, pickers, retail stores and even other resellers. They sell from many countries, but mainly sorting hubs in Pakistan, India and Dubai. They list through the Fleek Vendor app, and each listing is reviewed before it goes live.
- **Buyers:** vintage shops, online resellers (eBay, Vinted, Depop, their own stores), boutiques, and some larger retail chains and brands. Fleek is app-first on iOS and Android, with a website as well.
- **Business model:** marketplace take on transactions. The exact commission is not confirmed from an official source. Buyers pay the listed price (shipping included), plus a small-order fee and a buyer-protection fee where they apply.

### How the marketplace works (buyer side)

- **Formats:** bundles, bales and handpicks. Bundles start at about 10–20 pieces, and per-piece prices are shown up front. The homepage says "the more you buy, the less you pay per piece". Sample prices seen on the homepage ranged from about $5 to $37 per piece, for example Y2K tops at $4.99/pc and Coach bags at $37.28/pc during a sale.
- **Listing types:**
  - *Representative:* photos show the type of items; exact pieces vary.
  - *Exact:* what you see is what you get.
  - *Custom handpick:* items chosen specifically for you, often on a video call.
- **Grades** ([Grading Guidelines](https://support.joinfleek.com/hc/en-us/articles/42547096235803-Grading-Guidelines-and-Policy)):
  - **A, almost new:** little or no visible wear.
  - **B, moderately worn:** small stains or holes under 1 cm, missing buttons, broken zips.
  - **C, clearly worn.**
  - **Mixed bundles:** AB is about 70% A and 30% B; BC is about 60% B and 40% C; ABC is about 30/40/30.
  - **Margin of error:** 10% across a bundle.
  - **Category rules:** rips in workwear or denim may exceed 1 cm; up to 2–3 non-removable stains under 0.3 cm still count as B; branded items missing labels count as B.
- **Video handpicks:** the buyer books a live video call with a supplier and picks items in real time. Calls are recorded and used in disputes. Buyers are told to confirm grading during the call.
- **Demand Hub:** buyers post a sourcing request (brand, product type, quantity, grade), and suppliers respond with offers. Fleek describes it as flipping the model so "suppliers compete to fill your order". Suppliers see "Customer Demand" and "Wishlist" trend data in the Vendor app.
- **Make an Offer:**
  - The buyer submits a price on an eligible product (not all products are eligible).
  - The supplier accepts, counters or declines in the Vendor app within 48 hours, after which the offer expires.
  - Once accepted, a unique code is generated, and the buyer redeems it at checkout.
  - Buyers can re-offer after a decline or expiry.
- **Buyer Protection:**
  - It covers items not received, not as described (against the stated grade) and not authentic.
  - Fleek first raises the issue with the supplier. If that fails, Fleek gives a partial refund, or a full refund plus free return shipping if the whole order is not as described.
  - Claims must be filed within 5 days of receipt, with photos, via in-app chat, web form or email.
  - Returns must include the whole order unless the seller agrees otherwise.
  - Change-of-mind returns are generally not supported.
  - The fee is reported as 2% of the post-discount order total, capped at £20. That figure comes from a third-party review (SaveMyCent) and is not confirmed on an official page I could read.
- **Cancellations and refunds:** orders can be cancelled until they clear quality checks. Refunds take 2–3 business days before shipment, and 4–7 business days (plus bank time) after shipment once verified.
- **Shipping and customs:**
  - Shipping is included in the price, and Fleek ships to 90+ countries (homepage).
  - Delivery time is **inconsistent across sources**: "typically 10–14 working days" in Fleek's own Reseller Guide PDF, versus "21 to 28 working days, with sea and air options" in a third-party review. Customs clearance can take 60–90 days "in rare cases" (help centre).
  - Customs are pre-paid for most routes: for UK and US buyers when the seller is in Pakistan or India, and for EU buyers from Pakistan, India or EU sellers. US tariffs are bundled into the checkout price.
  - Fleek reimburses the non-VAT portion of customs where a buyer is wrongly charged.
- **Other buyer features:**
  - Buy Now, Pay Later up to 30 days after receipt (third-party review, not verified).
  - A referral programme.
  - A first-order discount code (Reseller Guide).
  - An account manager for larger buyers (third-party review).
  - Seasonal content such as an "AW26 trend report" and a "State of Resale" report on the homepage.
- **Pricing tiers:** I found no public buyer membership or subscription tiers. Pricing appears to be per listing, with volume discounts and fees at checkout. If the event materials mention tiers, treat that as newer than this research.
- **Homepage snapshot:** when fetched, the homepage showed a "Fleek's Biggest Sale of the Year / Black Friday, up to 50% off" banner. That is odd for September and may be cached or a promotional test, so don't quote it as current.

### Supplier side (Vendor app)

- **Sign-up and listing:** suppliers sign up with a phone number and can "explore live trending demand and discover local suppliers before you commit". Listings take a title, description, category, price and images, then Fleek reviews them before they go live.
- **Orders and payouts:** orders are tracked from creation to delivery, and the app promises "fast payouts".
- **Seller Tools:** campaigns, discounts and stocklist management.
- **Demand data:** Customer Demand and Wishlist insights to plan inventory.
- **Buyer contact:** chat and video handpicks.
- **Other apps:** a separate "Fleek QC App" appears on Google Play under the same developer.

## AI and agent work

- **Fleek Sort:**
  - What it is: a custom vision-language model trained in-house on about four years and "millions" of Fleek's own transactions.
  - Inputs: photos from an ordinary smartphone (graders capture about three images; one front photo can work). They are extending it to video.
  - Outputs: brand, style, category, condition and defects (rips, faulty seams), an estimated clearing price, and an estimated time to sell. Fleek's own post says "reads brand, category, condition and price from a photo in under a second".
  - Deployment: used by graders in sorting hubs in Pakistan, India and Dubai, with pilots in the UK, Europe and US. Processed items are listed on the marketplace automatically.
  - Why in-house: Fleek says it tried frontier models first and they failed on "latency, cost and accuracy" at warehouse volumes.
  - Authentication: the model catches obvious fakes, but human quality checks in Karachi and Delhi cover frequently counterfeited brands and categories.
  - Future: they are studying conveyor-belt use but say robotic sorting at human speed is "still a ways off".
- **AI pricing, search and matching** connect graded inventory to buyers (BPI announcement). Fleek describes every transaction as feeding back into the model.
- **External agents:** Alex Nikityuk says Fleek is "building AI agents that help buyers and suppliers communicate, reducing tasks that once took days to just minutes" (Fleek LinkedIn, Aug 2026). He also names logistics, marketplaces, AI agents and hyper-personalisation as their hard problems.
- **FleekOS (internal):**
  - A single internal platform where non-engineers build, deploy and share tools connected to company data. Auth, data and deployment are handled, and tools can be opened up to customers and suppliers.
  - They standardised on Anthropic models.
  - An "AI-PRD review gate" using Claude skills (prd-coach) scores specs and checks them against existing tools to stop duplicate work.
  - Claimed result: 533 PRs by non-engineers in a couple of months (Alex Nikityuk, Apr 2026).
  - Sanket Agarwal described it as "a collection of agents and tools with humans in the loop".
- **Fleek x a16z hackathon (11 July 2026, Fleek HQ):** Fleek ran its first hackathon two and a half months ago, with 100+ participants and 37 teams.
  - Challenge areas:
    - vision (VLMs for cataloguing, grading, authentication and visual search)
    - agents and LLMs (sourcing, pricing, negotiation, CX and ops automation)
    - a "circular moonshot"
  - Winners:
    - **1st:** a live-video app that guides a worker to film the right garment regions for better, faster grading, plus a FashionCLIP "fingerprint" that re-identifies a garment downstream without tags or RFID.
    - **2nd:** an AI auctioneer for live auctions ("TikTok Live for Fleek").
    - **3rd:** AI-native matching of buyers to suppliers and products based on their stores.
  - Judges included Alex Nikityuk, both co-founders, and people from a16z, Cursor, NALA, V7 and Custodian.
  - This shows what Fleek has already seen. Ideas close to these may read as less original to Fleek judges (inference).

## What they'd likely value as judges (inference)

All of the following is inference from public material.

- **Real secondhand-trade problems over generic retail ones.** Their framing is that "every item is one-of-a-kind: a single photo, no SKU, no clean data", and that new retail is solved while secondhand is wide open.
- **Workflow compression they can measure.** Examples are days to minutes for buyer–supplier communication, or grading throughput. Alex's background is in finance, so he may press on unit economics and whether a claimed metric is real.
- **Awareness of what Fleek already has:** Make an Offer, Demand Hub, video handpicks, Buyer Protection and Fleek Sort. A pitch that reinvents one of these without knowing it exists may land poorly. One that extends or connects them may land well.
- **Trust and verification:** grade accuracy, authenticity, dispute evidence (recorded calls, photos). Their whole origin story is about scams.
- **Agents that work alongside humans with a clear handoff**, consistent with "agents and tools with humans in the loop".
- **Pragmatic AI engineering:** cost, latency and accuracy trade-offs. They moved off frontier models for grading for exactly these reasons.
- **Novelty relative to their July hackathon:** live-video grading, garment fingerprinting, AI auctions and buyer–supplier matching were the top three.

## How it could relate to our hack

This is a neutral mapping, not a recommendation.

- **Buyer Experience:** helping a reseller judge a bundle before buying. Examples: interpreting grade mix and the 10% margin, comparing per-piece economics, and checking supplier ratings.
- **Merchant Tooling:** supplier-side tools such as drafting listings from photos, answering buyer questions, responding to Demand Hub requests or offers, and demand and wishlist insight. Buyer-side (reseller) tools also fit, such as restock planning from what sells on their Depop, Vinted or Shopify store.
- **New Ways to Buy:** negotiation (Make an Offer is a real mechanic with counters, a 48-hour expiry and checkout codes), sourcing requests, group buying, or live and auction formats (note: an AI auctioneer came second in July).
- **Agentic Commerce:** an agent acting for a reseller or supplier against a Fleek-shaped catalogue. It could search bundles, post sourcing requests, make and counter offers within spending rules, and assemble Buyer Protection claim evidence. This would need mock data, because there is no public Fleek API.
- **Storefront Experience:** visual search or discovery across one-off items with no SKU.
- **Practical notes:**
  - We are building in their office, and their Head of AI judges.
  - Using realistic Fleek terms (grades A/B/C and mixes, representative vs exact listings, handpicks, Demand Hub, Buyer Protection) would signal commerce depth.
  - Claims about their internals, such as fee levels or delivery times, are inconsistent across sources, so avoid stating them as fact on stage.
